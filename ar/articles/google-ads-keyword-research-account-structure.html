Google Ads keyword research: turn intent into an account structure

A practical sequence for researching search terms, grouping intent, and matching keywords to landing pages.

By Ayoub YatoujiPublished November 27, 2026Updated November 27, 20262 min read

Information last checked: November 27, 2026

Google Ads features, keyword metrics, and campaign controls can change. Verify current details and forecasts in the official account and documentation.

A useful paid-search structure connects what a person searches for to a relevant ad and landing page. Keyword lists are inputs to that decision, not a substitute for understanding intent or checking the account's actual data.

Begin with the offer and the searcher

Write down the product, service area, language, audience, and conversion goal. Expand from genuine customer questions and product terminology, then use an authorized source such as Keyword Planner to research terms and available estimates.

  • Keep the source and date beside any metric.
  • Separate research ideas from terms approved for a campaign.
  • Group by meaning and landing-page fit rather than superficial wording.
  • Mark ambiguous or irrelevant terms for review.
  • Check whether the page fulfills the promise implied by the keyword and ad.

Build groups around coherent intent

A group should contain terms that can be served by a consistent message and destination. If terms imply different products, audiences, or stages of decision, splitting them may make the campaign easier to understand and report on. There is no universal number of keywords per group.

Treat forecasts as estimates

Keyword planning tools can show estimates based on their data and settings. They are not guarantees of traffic, cost, or results. Preserve assumptions about location, dates, match settings, and budget when discussing an estimate.

Before import

Review match settings, negative terms, ad policy, conversion measurement, and landing-page relevance in the current Google Ads interface. Do not bulk-upload an automatically generated structure without a person checking it.
